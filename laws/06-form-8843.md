# Form 8843 — Statement for Exempt Individuals and Individuals With a Medical Condition

**Scope:** Logic spec for Form 8843 (2025 revision), the companion statement nearly every F-1/J-1/M-1/Q student and scholar must file. Unlike Form 1040-NR, Form 8843 is NOT an income tax return — it is a **status statement** that determines whether days of U.S. presence count toward the Substantial Presence Test (SPT). Filing it incorrectly or not at all has immigration-adjacent and tax-residency consequences, not just a missed deduction.

---

## 1. Purpose and the SPT Mechanic It Controls

The Substantial Presence Test (SPT) is the default test for whether an alien is a U.S. resident for tax purposes: present ≥31 days in the current year AND ≥183 "weighted" days over a 3-year lookback (current year days × 1.0 + prior year × 1/3 + year-before-that × 1/6).

Form 8843 is how a taxpayer claims that certain days should be **excluded** from that count — most commonly because the individual was an "exempt individual" (student/teacher/trainee/professional athlete on an F/J/M/Q visa) for all or part of the year, or was unable to leave the U.S. due to a medical condition.

```
IF days_excludable_as_exempt_individual > 0 OR days_excludable_medical_condition > 0
   THEN Form 8843 MUST be filed to substantiate the exclusion, REGARDLESS of income level
```

**Citation:** Form 8843 (2025) instructions p.3, "Who Must File"; "Substantial Presence Test."

---

## 2. Who Must File — Decision Logic

```
IF taxpayer is an alien individual (not a foreign-government-related "A"/"G" visa holder,
   other than A-3/G-5)
   AND ( taxpayer was an "exempt individual" for any part of the tax year
         [i.e., F/J/M/Q student, J/Q teacher/trainee, or professional athlete temporarily
          present for a charitable sports event]
         OR taxpayer was unable to leave the U.S. because of a medical condition that arose
            while in the U.S. )
   THEN Form 8843 MUST be filed
      — INCLUDING if the taxpayer has ZERO U.S. income and no other filing obligation.
```

This is the single most important rule for the F-1 population: **having $0 of U.S.-source income does NOT excuse an F-1/J-1 student from filing Form 8843.** It is a status filing, not an income filing. This is a common and costly misconception — flag any F/J/M/Q visa holder in the data intake who believes "no income = no filing" and route them to the Form 8843-only path.

```
IF taxpayer_visa_type IN {F-1, F-2, J-1, J-2, M-1, M-2, Q-1, Q-2}
   AND taxpayer_present_in_US_during_tax_year
   THEN form_8843_required = TRUE  (independent of income and independent of whether
       Form 1040-NR is otherwise required)
```

Note the F-2/J-2/M-2 dependents (spouse and children) each file their **own separate** Form 8843 — a household of an F-1 student, spouse, and two children generally needs **four** separate Forms 8843, one per individual, each completing only Part I (dependents are not "students" so Part III does not apply to them — they qualify as exempt individuals as members of the exempt individual's immediate family under the regulations, but complete only the general information).
**Citation:** Form 8843 (2025) instructions p.3, "Who Must File"; p.1, Part I.

### 2A. Foreign government-related individuals — narrow exclusion
```
IF taxpayer holds an "A" or "G" visa, and is NOT an A-3 or G-5 class visa holder
   THEN taxpayer is NOT required to file Form 8843 (not applicable to typical F-1 population)
```

## 3. The Four (Five) Parts of the Form

### Part I — General Information (lines 1a–4b) — ALWAYS completed by every filer
| Line | Field | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1a | Visa type + date of most recent U.S. entry | e.g., "F-1, 08/15/2024" |
| 1b | Current nonimmigrant status as of last day of tax year (+ change date/prior status if changed) | |
| 2 | Country(ies) of citizenship during the tax year | |
| 3a/3b | Country(ies) that issued passport(s) / passport number(s) | |
| 4a | Actual days physically present in U.S. during 2025 / 2024 / 2023 | feeds the SPT weighted calculation |
| 4b | Days in 2025 claimed as excludable for SPT purposes | the actual exclusion claim |

**Attachment shortcut:** if Form 8843 is attached to Form 1040-NR, lines 1a–4a may be left blank IF the same information is provided on Schedule OI (Form 1040-NR) — in that case enter "Information provided on Form 1040-NR" on line 1a, and still complete line 4b and the rest of the form. If filed standalone (no 1040-NR), ALL of Part I must be completed.
**Citation:** Form 8843 (2025) instructions p.3, "Part I — General Information."

### Part II — Teachers and Trainees (lines 5–8) — J/Q visa, non-student only
Completed only by J-1/Q-1 teachers or trainees (not students).
- Line 5: academic institution where taught (teachers)
- Line 6: director of program (trainees)
- Line 7: visa type held in each of the preceding 6 calendar years (2019–2024)
- **Line 8 — the 2-of-6-year test:** "Were you exempt as a teacher, trainee, or student for any part of 2 of the preceding 6 calendar years?"
```
IF line_8 == "Yes"
   THEN the individual generally CANNOT exclude 2025 days as a teacher/trainee
   UNLESS ALL FOUR of the following apply (the "Exception"):
      1. Exempt as teacher/trainee/student for 3 OR FEWER of the preceding 6 years
      2. A foreign employer paid ALL 2025 compensation
      3. Present in the U.S. as a teacher/trainee in at least one of the preceding 6 years
      4. A foreign employer paid ALL compensation in each such prior year
   → ROUTE TO HUMAN REVIEW: this is a multi-year factual reconstruction, not a simple flag.
```
**Citation:** Form 8843 (2025) instructions p.3-4, "Part II," "Exception."

### Part III — Students (lines 9–14) — the F-1 core case
- Line 9: academic institution attended during 2025 (name/address/phone)
- Line 10: director of program (if applicable — Q visa cultural exchange)
- Line 11: visa type held in each of 2019–2024
- **Line 12 — the 5-year test:** "Were you exempt as a teacher, trainee, or student for any part of more than 5 calendar years?"
```
IF line_12 == "Yes" (i.e., 2025 is the 6th+ calendar year as an exempt F/J/M/Q individual,
                      counting each partial calendar year as a full year)
   THEN the student CANNOT automatically exclude days of presence as a student in 2025.
   An exclusion is still possible ONLY if the taxpayer attaches a statement establishing,
   by facts and circumstances, that they do NOT intend to reside permanently in the U.S.
   Relevant facts include (non-exhaustive):
     - maintenance of a closer connection to a foreign country than to the U.S.
     - whether affirmative steps toward lawful permanent resident (LPR) status were taken
   → ROUTE TO HUMAN REVIEW: "intent" determinations are inherently facts-and-circumstances
     and should NOT be auto-resolved by the engine. This is also the point at which a student
     is at meaningful risk of flipping to resident-alien status under the SPT.
```
- Line 13: "During 2025, did you apply for, or take affirmative steps to apply for, LPR status, or have a pending application to adjust status?"
```
IF line_13 == "Yes"
   THEN taxpayer must explain on line 14, AND
   → ROUTE TO HUMAN REVIEW / IMMIGRATION-COUNSEL FLAG: this fact independently disqualifies
     the taxpayer from the "closer connection to a foreign country" exception (Form 8840) and
     is a strong signal of residency-intent issues relevant to both Parts III and the broader
     NRA-status determination. Also cross-reference against the §SPT discussion in File 1.
```
**Citation:** Form 8843 (2025) instructions p.4, "Part III — Students"; p.3-4, lines 12-14.

### Part IV — Professional Athletes (lines 15–16)
Charitable sports event name/dates and benefiting charity's name/EIN; a statement verifying all net proceeds went to the listed charity must be attached. Not applicable to the F-1 student population — include in the engine for completeness/other visa categories only.
**Citation:** Form 8843 (2025) instructions p.4, "Part IV."

### Part V — Individuals With a Medical Condition (lines 17a–18)
- 17a: description of the medical condition that prevented departure
- 17b: date the taxpayer had intended to leave (before the condition arose)
- 17c: date taxpayer actually left
- Line 18: **physician's statement**, signed by the treating physician/medical official, certifying the condition was not pre-existing and that the taxpayer could not leave as intended.

```
Exclusion is DISALLOWED (even with a completed Part V) if ANY of:
   - taxpayer entered/returned to the U.S. FOR medical treatment (regardless of whether they
     intended to leave promptly after treatment but couldn't due to unforeseen complications)
   - the condition existed BEFORE arrival and the taxpayer was aware of it
   - taxpayer was initially prevented from leaving, later became able to leave, but stayed
     beyond a reasonable period to arrange departure
```
→ ROUTE TO HUMAN REVIEW: Part V requires a physician's original signature/statement — this cannot be completed by the engine and requires a document-collection workflow, not a pure data-entry flow.
**Citation:** Form 8843 (2025) instructions p.4, "Part V."

## 4. Filing Deadline and Standalone Filing Rules

```
IF Form 8843 is attached to a Form 1040-NR
   THEN due date = same as the 1040-NR (see below), mailed with the return to the address in
        the 1040-NR instructions. No separate signature block is needed (it's part of the
        signed return package).

IF Form 8843 is filed standalone (taxpayer has NO Form 1040-NR filing obligation — e.g., zero
   U.S. income)
   THEN:
     - Taxpayer MUST complete the address-in-country-of-residence / address-in-U.S. fields at
       the top of the form (these are left blank when attached to a 1040-NR)
     - Taxpayer MUST sign and date the form (the "Sign here" block applies only to standalone
       filings)
     - Mail to: Department of the Treasury, Internal Revenue Service Center,
       Austin, TX 73301-0215, USA
     - Due date = the due date that would apply to Form 1040-NR for that taxpayer:
         IF taxpayer received wages subject to U.S. income tax withholding
            THEN April 15, 2026 (for TY2025)
         ELSE
            THEN June 15, 2026 (for TY2025)
       (Extensions of time, if validly requested, push this date out accordingly.)
```
**IF/THEN routing for the typical zero-income F-1 student (no on-campus job, no CPT/OPT income yet):** standalone Form 8843, due June 15, 2026, mailed to Austin TX — this is the most common single workflow LumaTax will process for first-year/low-income F-1 students.

**Citation:** Form 8843 (2025) instructions p.3, "When and Where To File"; Instructions for Form 1040-NR (2025), p.12, "When and Where Should You File?"

## 5. Consequences of Failing to File

```
IF Form 8843 is required AND not filed by the deadline
   THEN the taxpayer MAY NOT exclude the relevant days of presence (as exempt individual or
      medical-condition days) for SPT purposes
   → those days COUNT toward the Substantial Presence Test
   → IF this causes the weighted 3-year day count to reach 183+ (and current-year days ≥31)
         THEN the individual may be deemed a U.S. RESIDENT ALIEN for tax purposes for that
              year — losing NRA status, 1040-NR eligibility, and any treaty benefits that
              depend on NRA/treaty-residency status, and instead owing worldwide-income tax
              on Form 1040.
```

**Reasonable-cause exception:** no penalty/day-count-forfeiture applies if the taxpayer can show, by clear and convincing evidence, that they took reasonable actions to become aware of the filing requirement and significant steps to comply. This is a facts-and-circumstances defense, not a safe harbor the engine can auto-apply — flag any late/missed Form 8843 for human review rather than silently treating the taxpayer as having lost exempt-individual status.

**Citation:** Form 8843 (2025) instructions p.3, "Penalty for Not Filing Form 8843."

## 6. Common Mistakes (flag/validate against these)

1. **"No income = no filing" assumption.** Most common error for F-1 students with no job yet. Form 8843 is a status filing independent of income — see §2.
2. **Filing only one Form 8843 per family/household** instead of one per individual (spouse and each child each need their own). See §2.
3. **Not filing in subsequent zero-income years.** The obligation recurs every calendar year the individual is present in the U.S. under F/J/M/Q status and claims the exemption — it is not a one-time filing.
4. **Mis-answering line 8/line 12 (the 2-of-6 / more-than-5-years tests)** without realizing the cumulative year count includes partial calendar years and prior visits under any F/J/M/Q status (not just the current program). A student who did a prior J-1 summer exchange years earlier, then returns on F-1, must count that prior year.
5. **Forgetting to update Part I line 1b** when a status change occurred mid-year (e.g., B-2 visitor → F-1 student), including the change date and prior status.
6. **Treating Form 8843 as sufficient to claim the "closer connection" exception** — that is a DIFFERENT form (Form 8840) with its own test and is not interchangeable with Form 8843's exempt-individual exclusion.
7. **Omitting dependents (F-2/J-2) from the filing package** — each dependent present in the U.S. needs a Form 8843 even though they have no income and are not students themselves.
8. **Answering line 13 ("Yes," applied for LPR status) without realizing the downstream consequences** — this doesn't just affect Form 8843; it can affect the overall nonresident-alien determination and treaty eligibility. Always route to review (see §3, Part III).
9. **Using Form 8843 instead of Form 8840** when the taxpayer actually fails the SPT's exempt-individual exclusion path and needs the closer-connection exception instead (these serve different legal purposes: 8843 excludes SPT days as an exempt individual; 8840 claims closer connection despite meeting SPT). Confirm which pathway actually applies before selecting the form.

**Citation:** synthesized from Form 8843 (2025) instructions, pp.1-4, and Instructions for Form 1040-NR (2025), p.11 ("Exempt individual," "You can't be an exempt individual indefinitely").

## 7. Route-to-Human-Review Triggers (summary)

- Part II, line 8 = "Yes" (2-of-6-year teacher/trainee exception — multi-year factual test)
- Part III, line 12 = "Yes" (more than 5 calendar years as exempt student — intent-to-reside facts-and-circumstances test)
- Part III, line 13 = "Yes" (LPR application/affirmative steps — immigration-status flag, also disqualifies Form 8840 closer-connection path)
- Part V completed at all (medical condition exception — requires physician's signed statement, a document-collection workflow)
- Any filer whose cumulative F/J/M/Q exempt-individual years (current + prior nonconsecutive stints) is ambiguous or spans multiple visa types/program breaks
- Missed/late Form 8843 where the taxpayer may rely on the reasonable-cause defense (§5)
